# Policies and support

A course about performing a professional identity collapses the moment its
rules go vague, so these are short and plain. If you are reading this the
night before something is due, the heading you want is "Extensions and special
consideration" or "Late work".

## Academic integrity: framing, not fabrication

The course teaches framing, not fabrication. That single line is the boundary
everything else sits inside.

**Framing** is selecting, ordering and presenting things that are true so a
reader can see them: which three projects go on a CV, a README that says what
the thing does, a case study that opens on the problem. All taught here.

**Fabrication** is asserting something untrue, or letting a reader believe it.
Inventing a role, a grade, a date, a client or a technology. Inflating a
title. Claiming a team's output as yours, including by the careful use of "we"
where "I contributed to" is accurate.

Fabrication in course work is academic misconduct and goes to the university's
misconduct procedure. **Misrepresenting facts about yourself to an employer —
real or practice pool — is treated identically**, because a course that ruled
otherwise would be teaching fraud with a syllabus attached.

If you cannot tell which side something falls on, ask before you send it.
Asking is never held against you, and the answer is usually "say the true
version, it is stronger".

## Consent: the interview pool and outreach

Nobody outside this course is conscripted into it.

- **Practice Interview Partner Pool.** Every pool interviewer has given signed
  written consent, knowing they are conducting a SlopU practice interview for
  a course in which the interview is a teaching exercise. Nobody is deceived,
  and nobody unconsenting is approached. [Margot Tse](/people/margot-tse/)
  coordinates the pool and holds the paperwork.
- **You may not recruit your own pool interviewer** under a pretext. If you
  know a willing practitioner, send them to Margot to be consented properly.
- **Outreach (week 7).** Drafting the three messages is compulsory; sending
  them is your choice, and the Outreach Log is graded identically either way.
  Never misrepresent who you are or why you are writing, and do not contact
  someone twice about the same thing for the sake of a log entry.

## If you have no interview and no offers

Nothing happens to your mark. Nothing at all.

The [capstone](/assessments/04-capstone-applications/) is graded on process
artefacts only — never on outcome, never on whether you got an offer, never on
the prestige of the organisation. With no confirmed real interview by week 9
you opt into the practice pool, the debrief is graded identically, and no
marker sees which route you took. A semester of rejections, documented
honestly, is a strong capstone. That is not encouragement; it is the rubric.

## Extensions and special consideration

Ask **before** the due date wherever you can, and ask late if you could not.

- **Up to five days:** email [Margot Tse](/people/margot-tse/) with a sentence
  about why. Routine, and granted without documentation.
- **Longer, or special consideration:** apply through the university's process;
  Margot will help you lodge it.
- Illness, caring responsibilities, changed shifts and the ordinary collapse
  of a bad fortnight are all normal grounds. So is an application process that
  is making you unwell.

## Late work

Work submitted without an approved extension loses **5% of that assessment's
available marks per calendar day** from the deadline. After **seven days** it
is not accepted and the assessment is recorded as incomplete. With an approved
extension there is no penalty. Submitting something incomplete on time beats
submitting nothing.

## Generative AI

Permitted as a drafting and critique tool. Not permitted as a source of facts
about you.

- **Fine:** rewriting your own sentences, pressure-testing a criteria
  response, generating interview questions to practise against.
- **Not fine:** generating experience, projects, metrics or anecdotes you did
  not have; generating a peer critique and submitting it as one you wrote; a
  reflection entry for a week you did not attend.
- **Declare it.** Each submission carries a two-line note on where you used a
  model and for what. The declaration is not marked.

An AI-written claim about your own history is fabrication, regardless of who
typed it.

## Privacy: the Dossier and peer critiques

Your Dossier is coursework, not a public document. It is read by your marker
and, where you swap or submit it, by the specific peers involved — never
circulated, published, or used as a teaching example without your written
permission each time.

Peer critiques are visible to their author, their recipient and the marker.
Critiques you received are yours alone and are never assessed. Redact
salaries, client names and referees' contact details before submitting
materials as sent; a redaction never costs marks.

## Attendance and cross-stream visits

Tutorials are not attendance-marked, and no assessment here awards marks for
attending or for speaking in a room — including
[A3](/assessments/03-peer-critique/), which is graded on written critiques
only. You will still lose a great deal by not turning up, because four weeks
produce evidence you cannot manufacture alone.

Cross-stream attendance is encouraged and never assessed; tell the convenor
you are coming so the room has a chair. Weeks 6 and 12 open the stream walls
for everyone.

## Raising a problem

- **A specific session, or something taught badly:** tell that stream's
  convenor. The week-1 promise stands — if a session seems to be teaching
  fabrication, you have misread it or we taught it badly, and either way we
  want to know.
- **Something a convenor said or did, or anything escalated:**
  [Dr Wren Halloway](/people/wren-halloway/), course convenor.
- **Anything involving your wellbeing, the pool, or a process you would rather
  not discuss with your marker:** [Margot Tse](/people/margot-tse/).
- **Anything you would rather not raise with course staff at all:** the
  university's student advocacy service. Nobody on the teaching team is told
  you went.
