Nobody assesses your work. They assess your work as presented. That is the
uncomfortable premise of the midpoint lecture, and Nadia Ferrante — who reviews
portfolios for a living and can name the slide where she stopped reading —
delivers it without softening.

Everything in your Dossier is already varnished. The question was never whether;
it is thickness. Thin varnish makes real work legible: a README that says what
the thing does, a case study that names the problem it solved, a brief that
states its own constraint. Thick varnish conceals that there is not much
underneath, and people who hire for a living can tell by touch.

## The deck

This lecture runs almost entirely from slides, because the argument needs two
versions of the same artefact side by side and that is a slide rather than a
paragraph.

**[Varnish, and How Much Is Too Much](/decks/week-06-varnish/)** — thirteen
slides. It covers the ninety-second read as four questions an assessor actually
runs; four thin-varnish moves, each a sentence rather than a redesign; the four
tells of thick varnish, mirrored deliberately against those moves; the same four
moves applied across three surfaces, one per stream; a live teardown of one
artefact with its varnish annotated; the integrity boundary around the word "we";
and the anatomy of a Varnish Note entry that is worth writing.

Read it before the [gallery walk](/sessions/week-06/) if you want your critique
of other people's walls to be useful, and after it if you want to understand
what happened to yours.

## What the deck will not do for you

It will not tell you where your own line sits. Finish is field-specific — the
plainness that reads as confidence on a repository front page reads as
carelessness in a portfolio, and the sequencing instinct a designer treats as
craft reads as spin in a criteria response. The cross-stream lap at the end of
the tutorial exists because you cannot see your own conventions until you watch
another field break them without consequence.

The closing slide is the sincere sentence inside the joke, and it is worth
carrying to Assessment 2: varnish is not a lie. It is a claim about where to
look. Your Varnish Note is where you own that claim in writing.
