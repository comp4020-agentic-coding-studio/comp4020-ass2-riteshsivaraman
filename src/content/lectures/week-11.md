You will be hired before you can do the job. Everyone is. The interval between
those two events is competence lag, it is universal, and it is almost never
mentioned at the offer stage — which is why graduates spend their first quarter
convinced the gap is personal and secret.

It is neither. Managers budget for it, name it in their own heads, and are
mostly waiting to see how you handle it rather than how long it lasts. This
lecture is about handling it.

**Asking for help without forfeiting credibility.** The form is stable across
every field: what you were trying to do, what you tried, what happened, what you
think is going on, and the specific thing you want. Five clauses. A question in
that shape reads as competence with a gap in it. The same question without the
middle three reads as competence lag being outsourced, and people stop answering
by the fourth time.

**Learning in public.** The instinct is to study privately and emerge finished.
That maximises the lag, because you learn the wrong things unobserved, and it
looks from outside like a very quiet person producing nothing for six weeks.
Doing the learning where people can see it converts your lag from a rumour into
a legible trajectory.

**The ninety-day ramp.** Not the one HR gives you. Yours, built from your own
gap map, with the three tiers intact: what you close before you start, what
closes by asking, what closes only by doing. A ramp is useful in proportion to
how specific it is, and specificity is uncomfortable, which is why most people's
ramps are aspirations with dates attached.

The uncomfortable corollary, said once and not softened: pretending you have no
lag is the fastest way to lengthen it. Every hour spent performing fluency is an
hour not spent acquiring it, and the performance has a shelf life of about three
weeks in a team that works together daily.

[Today's tutorial](/sessions/week-11/) brings practitioners into each stream
room to say what your first ninety days would actually look like. They are
answering on a swapped Dossier, which is the arrangement that gets you the
honest version.
