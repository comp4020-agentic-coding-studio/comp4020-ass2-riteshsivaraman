An interview has two halves and only one of them is yours. This lecture is about
drawing the line between them accurately, because students routinely draw it in
the wrong place and then spend a fortnight rehearsing the weather.

**Yours:** the structure of your answers, the three anecdotes you brought, the
questions you ask, and your recovery from the one answer that lands badly.
That is a short list and it is fully preparable.

**Not yours:** the panel's mood, whether they read your materials this morning
or in the lift, the format nobody warned you about, the internal candidate, the
question that is really about a fight two people in the room had last Tuesday,
and the outstanding person interviewed at eleven. This is interview weather. It
is real, it changes outcomes, and it is not information about your worth.

The lecture works through the mechanics of each controllable in turn. Structure:
answering the question asked before answering the question you prepared for.
Examples: indexing three anecdotes against the question types, rather than
hoping the right one surfaces under pressure. Questions: four real ones, at
least one of which you would be uncomfortable hearing a bad answer to, because
that is the only kind that tells you anything.

Recovery gets the longest section, because it is the most underrated and the
most trainable. One bad answer does not sink an interview; visible collapse
after one bad answer does. The sentence that repairs it is short, is said
immediately, and is not an apology.

The last ten minutes are administrative and they matter. Everybody declares an
interview route at the end of [today's tutorial](/sessions/week-09/). If you do
not have a real interview confirmed, you opt into the Practice Interview Partner
Pool and you are not behind — you are on the other route, which is graded
identically. The pool exists because hiring calendars do not align with teaching
calendars, and never did.

Margot Tse coordinates the pool and its consent paperwork, and is the person to
see if any part of your application process is currently making you feel unwell.
That last offer is sincere, and is made every year because every year it turns
out to be needed.
