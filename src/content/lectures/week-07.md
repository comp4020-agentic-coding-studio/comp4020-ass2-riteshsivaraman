Cold outreach barely works and everybody involved knows it, which is why the
advice to send forty messages is advice to waste a fortnight. This lecture is
about the alternative: one stranger, pre-warmed, meaning somebody you have never
met who nevertheless has a specific reason to reply because you did the reading
first.

The distance between those two messages is usually a single sentence — the one
proving you know which thing this person made. Everything else is overhead.

## Four parts of a message that gets answered

**Who you are**, in one clause, accurately. **The warming sentence**, naming the
specific work and carrying a judgement about it rather than praise. **One
question**, answerable in a line by somebody reading on a phone between
meetings. **An exit** — a sentence that makes not replying costless.

That last part is the one students cut, and it is the one that makes the message
ethical rather than merely effective. A request engineered so that silence feels
rude is pressure wearing manners.

## Honesty, stated as a constraint

Never misrepresent who you are or why you are writing. You are a student, you
are doing this partly for a course and partly because you want work, and both
halves are entirely sayable. Inventing a mutual acquaintance, a shared project or
a pretext is not a technique this course teaches and is handled under
[policies](/policies/). The point of the course is framing, not fabrication.

## Sending is your decision

Margot Tse states this in the lecture and it is not softened in the
[tutorial](/sessions/week-07/): drafting three messages is compulsory, sending
them is your choice. The Outreach Log records what you drafted, what you sent,
what you deliberately did not send, and why. It is graded identically either way,
on the reasoning rather than on the traffic. An assessment that conscripts a
stranger into somebody's coursework is not an assessment this course is willing
to run.

## Silence

Most messages get none. Good ones get none. Silence is the standard reply, not a
verdict and not information about you — inboxes are weather. Expect it, budget
one short follow-up, and then stop, because the thing after that stops being
outreach.
