Wren Halloway opens the semester by listing three parts of her own job she is
still performing, with dates. It takes four minutes and it is not a warm-up
exercise; it is the argument.

## The premise

Everyone in the theatre is already performing a professional identity. You do
it when you choose which two of your four jobs go on a CV. Your tutors do it
when they answer a question confidently in an area they read up on last night.
The people who will interview you do it continuously, and are better at it than
you because they have had longer.

The course does not propose that you start performing. It proposes that since
you are already doing it badly and accidentally, you might do it deliberately
and well.

## The one rule

**Framing what is true is professional practice. Claiming what is not is
fraud.** There is no interesting grey zone between them, only an uncomfortable
one, and the discomfort is where the semester lives.

The lecture works through four cases at the boundary: the group project
described with "we", the six-week contract rounded up to "a semester", the
technology you used once and listed without qualification, and the interest you
named because the ad named it. Two of those are framing. Two are not. You will
disagree with which, and the disagreement is more instructive than the answer,
so bring it to your tutorial.

## Truthfloor

Your truthfloor is the lowest claim you could make about yourself and still
defend it in a room containing somebody able to check. Most CVs are written a
full storey above it. That is survivable right up to the follow-up question,
and the follow-up question is not optional — it is the entire second half of
every interview.

This week you find your floor. Every week after this one builds upwards from
wherever it turned out to be, which is why we do this before you write a single
word of new material.
