Every document you send carries a finite quantity of unevidenced claim before
none of it is believed. That quantity is your plausibility budget, and the
critical property is that it is **shared**: one indefensible line takes the true
sentence beside it down as well. An assessor who catches a claim you cannot
support does not quarantine it. They re-price the whole page.

Wren Halloway and Anselm Pike deliver this one together, on the grounds that it
needs both a hiring-panel view and a view from the person who has to read the
repository afterwards.

## Spending it deliberately

The mistake is not having a stretch in your materials. The mistake is having
four, scattered, unmarked, all cheap. The instruction is to pick the one stretch
that genuinely matters to this target, spend the budget there, and evidence
everything else down to your truthfloor.

Which means the work is subtractive. Receipt debt from week 4 is paid down by
cutting, narrowing, or attaching evidence — usually cutting, and usually the
sentence you were proudest of.

## Four things that overspend it

Adjectives standing in for outcomes. Ownership hidden in the passive voice.
Vocabulary with no depth one layer down from the noun. A stated result with no
number and no measurement behind it. Each is a tell the
[tutorial](/sessions/week-08/) will put a stopwatch on.

## Reading somebody else's budget

Half this lecture is the other direction, because the skill generalises further
than the assessment does. Once you can see your own budget you can see an
employer's — the job ad claiming a collaborative culture with no example, the
recruiter describing a role in adjectives, the start-up whose stated scale does
not survive one arithmetic question. You are allowed to run the ninety-second
read on them. Very few graduates think to.

## Where this lands

Four days before Assessment 2. The tutorial finds your weakest claim and times
how long it took; the week that follows is for acting on the number rather than
arguing with it. What you submit is the revised materials plus a diff note
naming what was cut and why, and a claim you kept unevidenced on purpose,
declared as such, costs you nothing.
