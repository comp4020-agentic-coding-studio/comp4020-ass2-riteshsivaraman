One question, eight minutes, your own stream. The question was published in week
one and has not changed: **which part of your performance became true, and how
do you know.** There is no second question and no supplementary reading. The
whole difficulty is in the second clause.

## The format

Your convenor and two peers from your stream sit the panel. You get two minutes
uninterrupted, then six of examination, and the examination is entirely about
evidence. "I'm much more confident now" is not an answer. "In week 3 I wrote
that I had never deployed anything other people depended on; here is the thing,
and here are the two people who depend on it" is an answer, and takes about the
same amount of breath.

Cross-stream observers are admitted and encouraged. Watching a commercial
student defend a claim to Sunil Raman, if you have spent twelve weeks in the
technical room, is the fastest available correction to the belief that your
field's conventions are the natural ones.

## What you are actually being asked

Three things, in this order:

- Name one claim from your [week 1](/sessions/week-01/) Baseline Inventory that
  was scaffolding then and is load-bearing now, with the receipt.
- Name one that is still scaffolding. Say so plainly. Nobody has ever lost marks
  in this room for that sentence, and several people have lost them for avoiding
  it.
- Say what you are going to do about the second one, on a timeline with a date
  in it.

That is the seamwork. The join between performed and genuine is not shameful and
it is not permanent; it is simply where the work goes next, and knowing where it
is beats pretending the surface is continuous.

## Leaving with

Dossier §13: the performance-versus-genuine audit, and with it the Dossier
closed — thirteen sections, twelve reflection entries, and a folder of receipts
for a person you have partly become. Keep it after submission. It is the only
document you will own that records what you could not do in February, and in
about two years that will be the useful part.
