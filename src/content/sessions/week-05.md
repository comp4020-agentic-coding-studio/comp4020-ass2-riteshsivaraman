Three stories, rehearsed until they bore you, is the whole ambition of the week.

## What you bring

Rough text for three experiences. Each needs four things written down before you
arrive: the situation, the decision **you** made, what followed, and what you
would change. Where one of the four is blank, bring it blank — the blank is the
teaching, and filling it in the room with something plausible is precisely the
habit being interrupted.

Bring Dossier §5 as well, your Receipt Ledger. An anecdote with nothing behind
it is a line item on your plausibility budget, and week 8 is somebody timing how
fast they can find it.

## The rotation

You work in threes and rotate every eight minutes. One of you asks from a deck
of the four question shapes a behavioural interview actually uses. One of you
answers, once, without restarting. The third observes and scores exactly one
thing: **did the story answer the question that was asked**, or did it answer an
adjacent question the speaker preferred.

That is a narrower brief than it sounds, and it is where the discomfort lives. A
well-told story about the wrong thing reads as evasion from the other side of
the table. The observer writes the verdict on the sheet rather than saying it,
because written critique is what
[Assessment 3](/assessments/03-peer-critique/) is assembled from and talk leaves
no receipt.

## The two failure modes your convenor will interrupt for

- **No decision in it.** Things happened and you were present. Nobody can score
  presence.
- **Someone else's outcome.** The team shipped, the campaign landed, the
  submission passed. Say what you did, then say what the team did, in that order
  and in the active voice.

## By stream

- **Technical Practice** — a failure or debugging story, scored on whether your
  reasoning under uncertainty is visible. How you narrowed it matters more than
  what the bug turned out to be.
- **Creative and Communications Practice** — a piece that did not work, and what
  the revision cost in hours, scope or goodwill. The cost is the part people
  remember.
- **Commercial and Public Practice** — a decision made on insufficient
  information, and who it affected. Name the group; "stakeholders" is reqspeak
  and a panel hears it as a dodge.

## Leaving with

Dossier §6: three anecdotes, each mapped to the four questions it answers
without buckling. Mapped, not merely written — the mapping is what makes any of
them load-bearing. The [lecture](/lectures/week-05/) gives you the structure;
this room tells you which of your three collapses under a follow-up.

[Next week](/sessions/week-06/) your work goes on a wall. Spend the days between
on the artefact, not on the stories.
