Chairs go into a horseshoe, one seat faces the rest, and you sit in it and read
your own claims out to a room that has been told to ask for receipts. Sunil
Raman designed this format and enjoys it more than he will admit.

## The procedure

You read one claim. Any peer may say "receipt". You have ninety seconds to name
the thing a stranger could check without you in the room — not to describe it
persuasively, to name it. If ninety seconds passes without one, the claim is
struck through on the spot and the tutorial moves on.

Three rules keep it a tribunal rather than a pile-on.

- **The claim is on trial, never the person.** You challenge a sentence, and
  you say which word in it you are challenging.
- **Struck is not deleted.** A struck claim goes into your ledger with the
  receipt it would need. Most of them are true and merely unevidenced, which is
  receipt debt, not dishonesty.
- **Everybody sits in the chair.** Nobody observes their way through this week.

Expect to lose between a third and half of what is on your current materials.
That is the median and it is not a bad afternoon.

## What counts as a receipt in your room

- **Technical Practice** — a commit, a thing that runs at a URL, or a
  measurement with a before and an after. "Familiar with" is not a receipt for
  anything.
- **Creative and Communications Practice** — a piece somebody other than you
  has seen, used, published, or paid for. Work seen only by your assessor and
  your flatmate has one receipt, not none, but say which.
- **Commercial and Public Practice** — a number, a decision you can name and
  date, or a stakeholder who would remember you. An outcome with no named
  decision inside it belongs to whoever ran the project.

## Two things you write before you leave

Your **Receipt Ledger** is the tribunal's transcript, formalised: each claim,
its verdict, and for anything struck, the specific artefact that would restore
it. Some of those artefacts take an hour to make, and you should make them
before Friday.

Your **rewritten Baseline Inventory** is Dossier §1 with the struck claims
removed rather than softened. Softening is the habit this week is built to
break: an unevidenced claim hedged into "some exposure to" is still spending
plausibility budget, and it now reads as though you know it is thin.

about where the gaps are, which is a thing you can finish by Friday.
