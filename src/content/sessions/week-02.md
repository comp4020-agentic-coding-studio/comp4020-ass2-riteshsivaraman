One advertisement goes up on the screen at the start of the hour and is still
up at the end, in four pieces. Bring the ad you actually want, not the one that
is easiest to read.

## Before you arrive

From the three ads you filed as Dossier §2, pick a primary target: the role you
would take on a Tuesday if someone offered it. Have the full posting with you,
not a recruiter's summary of it — the summary launders precisely the reqspeak
this session exists to read.

## The four columns

You rule up four columns and every line of the ad goes into one of them.

- **Boilerplate** — text present because the legal team or the applicant
  system requires it. Strike it. It carries no information about the job.
- **Wishlist** — the eleven technologies or attributes nobody expects in one
  human. Count them and write the number at the top. That number is a fact
  about the employer, not a verdict on you.
- **The work** — the sentences describing what you would be doing on a
  Wednesday afternoon. This column is almost always the shortest, and it is the
  only one your materials have to answer.
- **The confession** — the phrase that leaks what went wrong in the last hire.
  "Comfortable with ambiguity" means nobody has written the spec down.
  "Able to influence stakeholders at all levels" means the previous occupant
  could not.

No column is allowed to stay empty. If you cannot locate the confession, you
have not read closely enough yet, and your convenor will sit with you until it
surfaces.

## Your stream's document

The ads differ enough by field that the columns behave differently in each
room.

- **Technical Practice** works a graduate engineering ad, mapping each bullet
  to a verifiable artefact — the thing you would point at if the bullet were
  read back to you as a question.
- **Creative and Communications Practice** works a studio or agency brief and
  answers one question underneath the columns: what is actually being bought
  here, taste or throughput.
- **Commercial and Public Practice** works a written selection-criteria
  document and maps each criterion to the shape of answer it is fishing for,
  which is usually narrower than the criterion pretends.

## The swap, last twenty minutes

You hand your teardown to the person beside you and they mark one line they
would have filed in a different column. You argue it out. Column disputes are
the point — an ad that everyone reads identically was not worth tearing down.

## Leaving with

Dossier §3, a four-column annotated teardown of one primary target, each
annotation naming the column and the reason. Keep it legible by a stranger:
next week it is anonymised and handed to somebody who has never seen your ad,
and it travels into
[Assessment 1](/assessments/01-role-teardown/) largely as written.
