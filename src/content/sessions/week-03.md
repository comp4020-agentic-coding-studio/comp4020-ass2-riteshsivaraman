Your teardown from last week arrives at somebody else's desk with your name cut
off it, and theirs arrives at yours. Neither of you knows whose is whose until
the final fifteen minutes.

## What makes this work

Writing a gap map for your own target is an exercise in flinching. You know
which sentences you were hoping nobody would read closely, so you round them
down. Writing one for a stranger's target costs you nothing emotionally, which
is exactly why it comes out specific, and specific is the whole skill.

So: you receive an anonymised teardown, you write the gapshape you think the
absent author is facing, and only afterwards do you read the one written about
yours.

## What a gap map has to contain

Every gap gets a tier, and a gap without a tier is a mood rather than a plan.

1. **Closeable by week 12.** A fortnight of work, a small thing built, one
   course finished. Name the artefact that will prove it closed.
2. **Closeable inside a year.** Real, but not before you apply. This tier is
   what a ninety-day ramp plan gets built from in week 11.
3. **Not closeable, and therefore said out loud.** Three years of industry
   experience you do not have. This tier is the one candidates hide and panels
   find anyway. Said plainly and early, it costs you far less than it costs you
   discovered.

Write in the second person about the author you cannot identify. "You have
never deployed anything other people depended on" is usable. "Needs more
experience" is not a gapshape, it is a shrug.

## By stream

- **Technical Practice** reviews for technical-depth gaps: where vocabulary is
  running ahead of practice, and which claims would not survive a whiteboard.
- **Creative and Communications Practice** reviews for taste-and-range gaps —
  six pieces that are all the same piece, or a range so wide no point of view
  shows through it.
- **Commercial and Public Practice** reviews for judgement-and-context gaps:
  decisions described without their constraints, and outcomes described without
  the person who owned them.

## The reveal

You get your own map back and lay it beside the one you wrote about yourself.
The interesting entries are never the ones you agree on. Note where a stranger
saw a gap you had not written down, and where they missed one you had — a gap
invisible from your materials is not thereby closed, it is merely undisclosed,
and it will surface later at worse timing.

Your written review goes back to its author signed. Keep a copy; three of these
across the semester become your
[peer critique submission](/assessments/03-peer-critique/).

## Leaving with

Dossier §4, your Gap Map, every gap tiered, written against your own target and
corrected by what the blind review caught. Next week the claims that survived
your week-1 inventory get asked for receipts.
