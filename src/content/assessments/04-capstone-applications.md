The capstone is the applications you were going to make anyway, documented
properly. The course does not ask you to generate job-hunting activity for a
mark; it asks you to run the activity you already have with the instruments on.

## The facts

- **Submit** the closed Dossier as one bundle: the application materials as
  actually sent, the consolidated twelve-entry reflection log, one interview
  debrief, the ninety-day ramp plan, and the performance-versus-genuine audit.
- **Due** Wednesday 26 May 2027, 5:00 pm.
- **Worth** 45% of the course.
- **Marked** against the five weighted criteria at the foot of this page.

## Graded on process artefacts only

Plainly, and this is the first of the two places it is said on this page: **you
are marked on your process artefacts only. Never on the outcome. Never on
whether you got an offer. Never on the prestige of the organisation you applied
to.** A student who applied to two local employers, was rejected by both, and
documented the whole thing honestly can score higher than a student holding an
offer from a name you have heard of. That is not a consolation; it is how the
rubric is actually built, and you can check it against the criteria below.

## What goes in

- **Materials as sent.** The real versions, including anything you changed
  after [A2](/assessments/02-performance-materials/), and including the
  applications that went nowhere.
- **The reflection log**, consolidated — one entry per teaching week, twelve in
  total, written at the time rather than reconstructed in May.
- **One interview debrief.** What you controlled, what was weather, what you
  would do differently. See the route rules below.
- **The ninety-day ramp plan** from [week 11](/sessions/week-11/), built from
  your own gap map rather than from a template.
- **The performance-versus-genuine audit** from
  [week 12](/sessions/week-12/): which parts of the performance became true,
  how you know, and what you are going to go and learn.

## The week-9 safety net

Nobody in this course is required to have an interview in order to pass it, and
nobody outside this course is conscripted into it.

- **You apply only to roles you are actually pursuing anyway.** Applying to a
  role you have no intention of taking is not permitted. It wastes a real
  assessor's afternoon, and this course does not buy its marks with other
  people's time.
- **Every student declares an interview route** at the end of the
  [week-9 tutorial](/sessions/week-09/). The declaration is a Dossier artefact,
  not a judgement, and it is filed either way.
- **If you have no confirmed real interview, you opt into the Practice
  Interview Partner Pool**, coordinated by [Margot Tse](/people/margot-tse/).
  This is a normal route, not a fallback for the unsuccessful.
- **Pool interviewers have given signed written consent.** They are recruiters
  and practitioners who know they are conducting a SlopU practice interview for
  a course in which the interview is a teaching exercise. Nobody is deceived,
  and nobody who has not consented is approached.
- **The debrief is graded identically either way.** There is no mark difference
  between the two routes, and no requirement to disclose which route you used
  beyond the factual note in your log.
- **Misrepresenting facts about yourself to any employer, real or pool, is an
  academic integrity matter** and is handled under the
  [policies page](/policies/). To say it the way week 1 said it: the course
  teaches framing, not fabrication.

## How this is marked

Second and last time, beside the criteria themselves: **this assessment is
graded on process artefacts only — never on outcome, never on whether you got
an offer, never on the prestige of the organisation.** Every one of the five
criteria below is a question about what you did and what you recorded. None of
them is a question about what happened to you. Gap articulation under real
conditions, adaptation under real friction and completeness of the record are
all improved, not damaged, by a semester that did not go to plan.
