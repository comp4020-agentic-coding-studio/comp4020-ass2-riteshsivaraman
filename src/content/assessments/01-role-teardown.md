Four weeks in, you hand over the first third of your Dossier and find out
whether the floor you set in week 1 held once somebody else read it.

## The facts

- **Submit** Dossier §§1–4 as one PDF or one shared document, through the course
  submission point.
- **Due** Friday 19 March 2027, 5:00 pm (end of week 4).
- **Worth** 20% of the course.
- **Marked** against the four weighted criteria at the foot of this page.

## What goes in

Four numbered sections, in this order.

1. **Baseline Inventory (§1).** Every claim you currently make about yourself,
   each one paired with the evidence you could put in front of a stranger
   inside ninety seconds. Claims with no evidence stay in, marked as such. An
   inventory with nothing unevidenced in it is not an honest inventory, it is
   a second CV.
2. **Three target advertisements (§2).** Real, current, and for roles you would
   actually take. Screenshot or full text, with the source and the date you
   captured it.
3. **Annotated teardown (§3).** One of those three ads, pulled apart into the
   four columns from [week 2](/sessions/week-02/): legal boilerplate, the
   wishlist nobody expects met, the work you would actually do on a Tuesday,
   and the accidental confession about what went wrong in the last hire. Every
   line of the ad lands in exactly one column, and you say why.
4. **Gap Map (§4).** Every distance between the baseline and the target, each
   tiered: closeable by week 12, closeable in a year, or not closeable and
   therefore something you will say out loud in the room.

## Where it comes from

Nothing here is written the night before. §1 and §2 come out of the
[week-1 self-audit clinic](/sessions/week-01/), §3 out of the
[week-2 teardown](/sessions/week-02/), §4 out of the
[week-3 blind review](/sessions/week-03/), and the
[week-4 claims tribunal](/sessions/week-04/) is your last chance to have peers
strike an unevidenced claim before a marker does it for you.

## What separates a strong submission

The teardown is where most of the accuracy marks sit, and the gap map is where
most of the marks sit full stop — 40 of them. A gap map that says "I need more
experience" earns very little. A gap map that says "I have never deployed
anything other people depended on, which is tier two, and here is the specific
thing I am doing about it by week 12" earns most of the 40. The specific
version is harder to write and easier to respect.

Legibility is only worth 10, but it is the 10 you lose by accident. Someone who
has never met you reads this document without you narrating it. Number your
sections. Label your columns. Date your evidence.
