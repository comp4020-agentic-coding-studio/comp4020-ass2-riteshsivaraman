Half of what you learn this semester arrives in someone else's handwriting on a
sheet beside your work. This is the ten per cent that pays for the other half.

## The facts

- **Submit** three written critiques you gave, each with a change note and the
  recipient's countersignature, as one document.
- **Due** Friday 14 May 2027, 5:00 pm (end of week 11).
- **Worth** 10% of the course.
- **Marked** against the three weighted criteria at the foot of this page.

## This is not a participation mark

Read this paragraph before you read anything else on the page. **You are graded
on the written critiques you gave, and on nothing else.** Not on attendance.
Not on how much you said in the room. Not on whether you are the sort of person
who speaks first in a tutorial. A student who attends every session and writes
nothing scores zero here; a student who writes three excellent critiques scores
full marks regardless of how quiet they were while writing them.

There is a reason the evidence is written. Verbal critique leaves no receipt,
and a mark with no receipt behind it is exactly the thing this course spends
twelve weeks teaching you to distrust.

## What goes in

Choose the **three most substantial** critiques you gave across the semester,
from any of [week 3](/sessions/week-03/) (blind review),
[week 6](/sessions/week-06/) (gallery walk),
[week 8](/sessions/week-08/) (red team) or
[week 11](/sessions/week-11/) (artefact swap). For each one, submit:

- **The critique as you gave it**, unedited. Do not improve it now.
- **A short change note** — two or three sentences on what it changed in the
  recipient's work, or an honest account of why it changed nothing.
- **The recipient's countersignature**, confirming they received it and that
  your change note is accurate. A countersignature is confirmation, not
  endorsement; your peer is not being asked to say the critique was good.

## What separates a strong submission

Specificity carries 40 marks, and it means critique of a **decision**, not of
the finish on it. "The contrast on your headings is low" is a note about
polish. "You opened the case study with the solution, so the reader never finds
out what was hard about it" is a note about a decision, and it is the one the
author can act on.

Usefulness carries 35, and it is evidenced by what happened next. A critique
that was considered and deliberately rejected, with the author's reasoning in
the change note, scores as well as one that was adopted. What does not score is
a critique so general the recipient could not have acted on it either way.

Reciprocity and conduct carries 25. Across four critique weeks you both give
and receive. Critique the work, name the decision, and remember that the person
reading it has their own materials on a wall two metres away.

## Privacy

Critiques you received are yours and are never circulated. Critiques you gave
are read by your marker and by the recipient who countersigned them, and by
nobody else. The full rule is on the [policies page](/policies/).
