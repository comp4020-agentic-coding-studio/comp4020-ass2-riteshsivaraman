Prologue

A set you build before
anyone takes their seat

Most of what you will make in this course is scaffolding — the flats, the blocking, the run sheet. It is the part nobody in the audience is meant to notice, and it is the part that decides whether the thing holds together on the night.

So the studio runs like a production. Every week opens on a shared stage: one lecture, one problem, everybody in the same room looking at the same object. Then the room splits into three scenes running in parallel, each taking that week’s problem into a different kind of material. You commit to one scene for the semester and you watch the other two from the wings.

What you are graded on is the legible trace of your process — the annotated draft, the rejected version with a reason written on it, the decision you reversed and dated. Outcomes are noisy and mostly not yours to control. The record of how you worked is entirely yours, and it is the only thing here that can be read fairly.

Bring nothing but a way of keeping notes. Everything else is cut, folded and taped together in the room.

Twelve sessions · one shared lecture each · three parallel streams · one capstone assembled from work you were going to do anyway.

Scene I · the three streams

Same problem,
three materials

Stream A

Builds the object

Fabric &
Structure

You take the week’s problem and make a physical answer to it — something with load paths, tolerances and a way of failing. The critique is on whether the thing stands, and on whether your drawings match what you actually built.

  • Weekly build to a fixed material budget
  • A drawing set that stays in sync with the object
  • One deliberate failure test, documented

Studio · 3 hrs · bench space allocated

Stream B

Runs the room

Blocking &
Facilitation

Your material is other people’s attention. Each week you convene a short session on the shared problem and are assessed on the structure you gave it, not on whether the group agreed with you at the end.

  • A run sheet written before, marked up after
  • Two rounds of peer observation notes
  • One session you deliberately hand over

Seminar · 2 hrs · paired rotation

Stream C

Keeps the record

Archive &
Evidence

You work on the trace itself: what gets written down, how it is indexed, and what a reader can reconstruct from it a year later. The stream that most often changes how people in the other two streams work.

  • A working archive with a stated retrieval policy
  • Weekly citation audit of your own claims
  • One reconstruction of somebody else’s week

Desk work · 2 hrs · archive access

The thread is the point — every week the three scenes are stitched back to one lecture before they diverge again.

Nothing in the system checks that a course’s weightings add to one hundred. That is a human job, so this page makes the arithmetic physical: the three counters are the whole mark, the tray is the course, and you can see immediately when one is missing.
