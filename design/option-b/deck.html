Drift and
correction

Every instrument drifts. The useful question is not how to stop it, but how often you have to check — and what you are willing to accept in between.

Slides are a summary. The argument is in the readings and the audit.

02 · The distinction

Noise averages out.
Drift does not.

Noise is symmetrical, so more readings make it smaller. Drift is directional, so more readings make it more convincing. That asymmetry is the whole week.

03 · Telling them apart

Two errors that look identical in a single reading

Noise

  • 01Symmetrical about the true value.
  • 02Shrinks as you take more readings.
  • 03Visible in the spread, not the mean.
  • 04Cheap to live with. Report it and move on.

Drift

  • 01Directional, and usually slow.
  • 02Grows more persuasive with more readings.
  • 03Invisible unless you kept the early data.
  • 04Expensive. Every internal check still passes.
04 · What checking costs

From three semesters of student folios

0× / sem Median checks run

Four is enough for a twelve-week project. Fewer than two and drift goes unnoticed.

0min Median cost per check

Checks that cost more than an hour do not get run. Design to the half-hour.

0% Drifts caught by the first check

Most of the value is in having any check at all, not in having a clever one.

Read these as orders of magnitude, not as targets. The number that matters is yours.

05 · Where each channel drifts

Same reference, three different failure modes

Channel ADrift in the asking. You get better at the interview, so your early transcripts answer a question you no longer hold.
Channel BDrift in the instrument. Every fix is locally correct; the accumulation is not. The log is the evidence, not the build.
Channel CDrift in the record. Categories move across decades and nobody announces it. The finding aid looks stable and is not.

Bring your own channel’s material to the session. Read the other two before week eleven — the objections you will face come from the rows you skipped.

06 · The drift audit · 55 min · in pairs
  1. State the referenceOne sentence. No hedging clauses.5 min
  2. Find the earliest artefactOldest item made under it. Do not tidy it.5 min
  3. Mark the movementOn your partner’s material. Mark generously.15 min
  4. Rank by consequenceMost marks change nothing. Say so aloud.10 min
  5. Design the cheapest checkCatches it in a week, runs in half an hour.15 min
  6. Commit the checkInto the plan, with a date.5 min

The steps are ordered because each consumes the previous one’s output. Reordering them to save time is the most common way this exercise produces nothing.

07 · The claim to argue with

A method that cannot detect its own drift is not a method. It is a habit with documentation.

Bring an objection to this on Thursday. The strongest one so far came from Channel C: some records are worth keeping precisely because nobody maintained them, and a maintained archive is a corrected one.
