The fold — when rehearsal becomes record

Everything you have made so far was practice, until someone kept a copy. This week is about the moment the two stop being distinguishable, and what you owe the record once it exists.

§01

Shared lecture

A recording is not a neutral copy of a rehearsal. It selects, and the thing it selects becomes the thing that happened. Once you have accepted that, the interesting question is not whether to record but what you are prepared to have selected for you.

The lecture works through three cases where the fold ran the wrong way — where a working document was read as a finished claim — and then, more usefully, two where someone anticipated it and set the record up deliberately in advance.

Lecture spine

  1. 00:00What the fold is, with the shortest possible version of the argument.
  2. 00:09Three cases where a draft was read as a position, and what it cost each time.
  3. 00:23Two cases where the record was set up on purpose, before there was anything worth recording.
  4. 00:36The stranger test, demonstrated live on a volunteer’s draft.
  5. 00:46Handover to stream sessions, and what each one is doing differently this week.
§02

Stream sessions — same fold, three angles

A

The written record

Tue 14:00 · Room 2.14 · Bring a draft you have not shown anyone

You run the stranger test on your own draft, which is harder than running it on someone else’s because you cannot un-know the context.

The procedure is fixed: hand over the page, say nothing, and write down the first question verbatim. Do not answer it in the room.

Most drafts fail at the same place — the reader asks what the thing is, which means the first paragraph is doing something other than its job.

Leave withThree verbatim first questions and a one-line note on which of them you were expecting.

B

The spoken record

Tue 14:00 · Studio 1 · Bring headphones and last week’s transcript

Two takes of ninety seconds on the same prompt, with a fifteen-minute gap in which you are not allowed to write anything down.

You keep both recordings. The annotation happens afterwards on the transcript, not in the studio, and it is the annotation that comes to the next session.

The second take is usually shorter and worse-sounding. That is the expected result and it is not a problem to be fixed.

Leave withTwo recordings, one transcript, and the gap between them marked in the margin.

C

The made record

Tue 14:00 · Workshop B · Bring one finished object

The session is an excavation. You put one finished thing on the bench and the group works out what it actually demonstrates.

That is rarely what it was assigned to demonstrate, and the gap between the two readings is where the unclaimed evidence sits.

Bring the failed attempt too if you still have it. It carries more of the working than the version you were happy with.

Leave withA group reading of your object, written by someone else, that you have not corrected.

§04

The activity

Run the stranger test on yourself

Forty minutes, done alone before your stream session. It is not submitted and it is not marked, but every stream assumes you have done it.

  1. Choose a piece of your own work that you believe is finished.
  2. Write, in one sentence, what you think a reader will ask first.
  3. Hand it to someone with no context. Say nothing at all.
  4. Write their actual first question down word for word.
  5. Compare the two sentences. The distance between them is the week’s result.
