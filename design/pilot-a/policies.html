What the course actually enforces

Policies

Three things bind this course beyond the assessment ladder: what counts as evidence, what happens when a deadline is missed, and what happens to a student with no confirmed real interview by week nine. All three are written out here in full — none of them is a footnote.

The invented-record boundary

Week one names the course’s only rule out loud: the difference between framing what is true and claiming what is not. Every student sets their truthfloor in the week-one self-audit clinic — the lowest claim they could make about themselves and still defend in a room containing someone able to check — and every claim made afterwards, in the Dossier or anywhere else on the record, is held to it.

Week four’s claims tribunal is where that floor gets tested, not set: each student reads their claims aloud and peers demand a receipt for each. A receipt is a commit, a running thing, or a measurement in Technical Practice; a piece someone other than the author has seen or used in Creative and Communications Practice; a number, a decision, or a named stakeholder in Commercial and Public Practice. A claim that survives ninety seconds of a peer asking “show me” stays on the record. One that doesn’t is struck on the spot — that is a tutorial exercise, not a penalty, and being struck in week four costs nothing.

What costs something is the same act done for real, later, against an actual employer, real or pool. Misrepresenting facts about yourself to any employer — real or Practice Interview Partner Pool — is an academic integrity matter, not a style note. Rehearsing a role you do not yet hold is the entire point of the course; inventing evidence for a claim you cannot back is not rehearsal, it is the one thing the course refuses to teach. The course teaches framing, not fabrication.

Late submission and extensions

Each assessment carries a due date and time on its own page and on the assessment ladder; there is no grace window built into those times. Anyone who can see a deadline becoming a problem — a clash with a real application, a genuine emergency, anything that would otherwise force a choice between the Dossier and the job it is meant to be documenting — raises it with Margot Tse before the due time, not after. Extension requests are assessed on the same evidentiary standard as everything else in this course: state what happened and what is needed, plainly, and expect to be asked a follow-up question.

An approved extension moves the due time; it does not lower the bar the work is marked against. Work submitted late without an approved extension is marked as submitted, against whatever record exists at the time it lands — a partial Dossier section is marked as a partial Dossier section, not excused down to a lower weight class. Given that A4 alone is graded on the completeness and honesty of the process record (15 of its 45), an incomplete late submission mostly costs itself.

The week-9 safety net

This is a load-bearing ethics constraint, not a fallback nobody is meant to use.

  • Students apply to roles they are actually pursuing anyway. Applying to a role with no intention of taking it is not permitted.
  • By the end of the week-9 tutorial, every student declares an interview route. A student with no confirmed real interview opts into the Practice Interview Partner Pool, coordinated by Margot Tse.
  • Pool interviewers are recruiters and practitioners who have given signed written consent, knowing they are conducting a SlopU practice interview for a course in which the interview is a teaching exercise. Nobody is deceived, and nobody who has not consented is approached.
  • The debrief is graded identically either way. There is no mark difference between routes, and no requirement to disclose which route was used beyond the factual note in the log.
  • Misrepresenting facts about yourself to any employer, real or pool, is an academic integrity matter and is handled under these policies. The course teaches framing, not fabrication.

No student ever approaches an employer or recruiter without their knowledge and consent to the practice-interview arrangement, full stop — that consent basis is absolute and admits no exception.
