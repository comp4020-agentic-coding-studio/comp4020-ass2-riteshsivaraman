A studio course in professional identity performance: how a person assembles fluency in a career they do not yet hold, and where framing what is true stops and claiming what isn't begins. Twelve weeks of shared lectures, three field streams, and one Dossier built from applications you were pursuing anyway.

Assessed on process, not outcome

§01
Premise

Everyone in this room is already performing a professional identity, including the staff. The only rule is the difference between framing what is true and claiming what is not — and the course's one piece of jargon worth learning in week one is the truthfloor: the lowest claim you could make about yourself and still defend it in a room containing someone able to check. Most CVs are written a full storey above their author's truthfloor. That is survivable right up until the follow-up question.

Twelve weeks run one arc. The first four establish an honest baseline and teach you to read what a role is actually asking, underneath the advertisement's boilerplate. The next four build materials fitted to a real target and then attack them — your peers red-team your claims and time how long it takes to find the weakest one. The last four perform live: interview, negotiate, and audit afterwards what became genuine and what was only ever costume.

One artefact runs the whole semester. The Dossier gains one numbered section a week, is submitted in part twice, swapped with a peer once, and closed at week twelve as the capstone — graded on the process record it contains, never on whether an offer arrived, and never on the prestige of who made it.

Three field streams run the tutorials, split by how a field actually screens candidates rather than by faculty.

01

Technical Practice

For the fields where the performance is competence, and someone will eventually ask you to open a terminal in front of them.

Software, engineering, data and analytics, applied and environmental science, health and biomedical technology, IT operations.

02

Creative and Communications Practice

For the fields where the performance is taste, and the portfolio is both the evidence and the alibi.

Design, media production, writing and editing, marketing and communications, architecture, arts and cultural administration.

03

Commercial and Public Practice

For the fields where the performance is judgement, and nobody will ever ask to see the spreadsheet.

Consulting, finance, accounting, law, policy and public service, management, NGO and program delivery.

Pick by how your target roles screen you, not by what your degree is called. Find out more about the streams →

§02  Twelve weeks, read sideways week 01 / 12  ·  keep scrolling downswipe sideways
01

Truthfloor

Self-audit clinic. Bring three real job ads and whatever you currently have, and mark which claims survive today's evidence. Nothing is graded — the point is finding the floor.

02

Reqspeak

Live teardown of one ad into four columns. The fourth column — the accidental confession about what went wrong in the last hire — is the useful one.

03

Gapshape

Blind review, swapped within stream. Gaps are not sizes, they are shapes: closeable by week 12, closeable in a year, or must be said out loud.

04

Receipt debt

Claims tribunal: read your claims aloud, peers demand a receipt for each. Unevidenced claims are struck from the record on the spot. A1 due Fri 19 Mar.

05

The load-bearing anecdote

Paired roleplay, rotating. Three stories prepared properly beat twelve improvised — and preparing one is not the same as inventing it.

06

Artefact varnish

Gallery walk: work on the walls, written critique on sheets beside it. The flagship lecture and deck — thin varnish makes real work legible, thick varnish hides that there is little underneath.

07

Now → The pre-warmed stranger

Outreach sprint. Sending is the student's choice — the log records what was drafted, sent or deliberately not sent, and why, graded identically either way.

08

Plausibility budget

Red-team session within stream: peers attack submitted materials for the weakest claim and time how long it takes to find it. A2 due Fri 23 Apr.

09

Interview weather

Mock panel run to each field's convention. The safety-net week: every student declares an interview route, real or the Practice Interview Partner Pool.

10

The negotiation flinch

Negotiation simulation with a staff member playing the other side. The hard rule: every student says the number out loud at least once.

11

Competence lag

Artefact swap: complete Dossiers exchanged within stream, presented by someone other than their author to that stream's guest. A3 due Fri 14 May.

12

Seamwork

Closing viva: eight minutes per student, examined on which part of the performance became true, and how they know. Capstone due Wed 26 May.

§03  Four things this course refuses position 1/4
Refusal 01

We do not grade the outcome

The capstone is graded on process artefacts only: the reflection log, the interview debrief, the ninety-day ramp plan, the performance-versus-genuine audit. Never on outcome, never on offer, never on the prestige of the organisation. It says so twice — once in the brief and once beside the criteria.

Refusal 02

No non-consenting stranger

Week 7's outreach log records what was sent or deliberately not sent, graded identically either way — nobody is coached to cold-contact a stranger under false pretences. Week 9's Practice Interview Partner Pool runs on signed written consent from every interviewer, so no real recruiter is ever practised on without knowing it.

Refusal 03

No participation marks for talking

Assessment 3 is graded on the three most substantial written critiques a student gave, countersigned by the recipient — never on attendance, never on airtime in a tutorial. The most reversible call in the ladder, held to that standard on purpose.

Refusal 04

No invented record

Rehearsing a role you do not hold is the coursework. Misrepresenting facts about yourself to any employer, real or pool, is an academic integrity matter. The course teaches framing, not fabrication — every claim made here must survive a stranger checking it.
